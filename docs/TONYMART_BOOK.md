## Module 1 — The Boss Arrives

The store opened at eight. The Boss arrived at eight-fifteen, which she logged, in her own handwriting, as seven fifty-five. She had been doing this for eleven years. The logbook had become a work of speculative fiction, and she was its only unreliable narrator.

Her badge said MANAGER in a font she had chosen personally because it "sounded loud." She had laminated it twice — once for protection, once for emphasis. When she walked the floor, aisles seemed to straighten themselves. Employees evaporated into Stock. Somewhere, a price gun clicked nervously.

> Punctuality is a feeling, and I feel punctual.

Nobody argued. Arguing was a write-up. Agreeing too slowly was also a write-up. The write-up forms were kept in a drawer labeled MORALE, which no one had ever found funny except her, every single morning, for eleven years.

She did her morning walk in a pattern no one could predict, which she called leadership and the staff called the migration. It ended, as it always ended, at the schedule board — where, at eight-twenty, the schedule was discovered to be wrong. Everyone was scheduled. No one was scheduled. Both, somehow, at once.

The Boss approached the board making the face. Employees within a six-foot radius developed urgent tasks in distant departments. You did not move fast enough.

- **Fix the schedule quietly** — _Initiative. Interesting. That is a trait we monitor._
- **Pretend to be on a call** — _Smart. Unverifiable. HR respects that._

The Boss called a meeting to discuss the meeting she planned to call later. Attendance was mandatory. The meeting was about attendance. It ran long because she paused, twice, to make sure everyone was listening, which guaranteed that no one was.

Somewhere between agenda item one and agenda item one, she felt something unfamiliar flicker through her chest. It was not the coffee. It was almost — and she would deny this later, under oath if necessary — self-awareness. She checked her calendar and scheduled it for never.

## 📌 Memo

What no one knew, because she had never once said it out loud, was that The Boss kept a list in her office titled THINGS I DID WELL TODAY. Most days it had a single entry. Most days the entry was "showed up," written at eight-fifteen, logged as seven fifty-five.

But the list existed, and that mattered. Lists were how she loved things. It would take her eleven more chapters to learn how to love anything off-list, and she would fight it the entire way down.

By nine, the schedule crisis had resolved itself the way most crises did: an employee fixed it quietly, and The Boss recorded it as a personal victory, filed under L, for Leadership.

Eleven years of mornings exactly like this one. Eighty-eight pages to find out what they cost her. If you asked her today she would say: nothing. The drawer labeled MORALE says otherwise.

## Module 2 — The Suggestion Box

A suggestion box appeared outside the break room on a Tuesday. It was bolted shut. This was intentional. The Boss had ordered it from a catalog of "morale solutions" and had specifically requested the model without a working slot mechanism, which cost extra.

Employees left suggestions anyway, sliding folded papers into the seam at the top. The seam, it turned out, led nowhere. The box was solid wood. The suggestions accumulated in a thin geological layer on top, like snow on a roof that no one shoveled, until Thursday.

On Thursday, The Boss asked the floor a question she had never asked before in her career. "Am I," she said, slowly, like the words were a new product she did not trust, "a good listener?" She was not listening to the answer. Her pen was already moving.

**Decision — "What would you do?"**

> Her pen hovers over your name. How do you answer?

- **"Yes. Absolutely. The best."** — _Correct answer. It has been noted in your file, approvingly._
- **"Define listener."** — _Risky. The file grew thicker. Files only ever grow._
- **Say nothing at all** — _Silence is agreement. You have been promoted to Senior Silence Associate. No raise._

That night, alone in the office with the security monitors glowing like aquariums, The Boss pried open the suggestion box with a price gun. It took forty minutes. She would have asked maintenance, but maintenance would have asked why, and why was none of their business.

Inside the seam — wedged, compressed, fossilized — were forty-one suggestions. Forty of them said the same thing. She read all forty anyway, in order, under the desk lamp, the way other people read letters from home.

> They want to be heard. I want to be agreed with. Surely those are the same thing.

It was the closest she had come to a breakthrough in eleven years. She held the paper crane up to the lamp, turned it over twice, and felt the unfamiliar flicker again — stronger this time, almost a sentence.

Then she wrote herself up for unauthorized after-hours equipment use, filed it in the MORALE drawer, and went home. Growth, like the suggestion box, was bolted shut. But the crane rode home on her dashboard.

## Module 3 — The Customer Is Sometimes

The customer arrived at noon with a fern. The fern was enormous, clearly dying, and — this part matters — visibly tagged with the price sticker of a rival store. The customer wanted to return it. There was no receipt. There had never been a receipt. There would never be a receipt.

> The customer is always right, but only during posted hours.

You were the nearest employee. This was not your department. At TonyMart, your department was wherever The Boss was looking, and The Boss was looking directly at you from behind a tower of discounted candles, observing how you would handle it. This was, you understood instantly, a test no one would admit was a test.

The customer set the fern on the counter. A single brown frond detached and drifted to the floor with the gravity of a verdict.

**Decision — "What would you do?"**

> The fern sits between you. The Boss is watching. What do you do?

- **Accept the return, no questions** — _Generous. With store money. Noted._
- **Ask for the receipt, gently** — _By the book. The book thanks you. I remain neutral._
- **Page The Boss to handle it** — _Delegation, upward. A bold inversion. Your file is becoming a saga._

What happened next was not in any training module. The Boss emerged from behind the candles, looked at the fern, looked at the customer, and said: "I remember this fern." Everyone froze. The fern was not from this store. Everyone knew the fern was not from this store, including the fern.

She refunded the full amount in store credit, to a person who had never spent a cent here, for a plant that belonged to a competitor. Then she watered the fern.

## 📌 Memo

Later, in the break room, theories multiplied. She knew the customer. She feared the customer. She had once been the customer. The truth, which the book will spend three more chapters arriving at, was simpler and worse: she had looked at a dying thing nobody would take responsibility for, and recognized it.

> I gave that fern what everyone deserves: store credit and a second chance.

The fern lived in her office after that, next to the list of things she did well. Some mornings the list still said only "showed up." But now, underneath, in smaller handwriting, it sometimes also said: "watered the fern."
